# Maximising discovery in high-throughput proteomics experiments with power-aware R packages

*Dan MacLean*

Most biological experiments are underpowered and most biologists don't know it. At high-throughput scale this means whole swathes of real biology go undetected, yet the habits of three replicates and *p* < 0.05 persist as a rule and are rarely changed. Consideration of statistical detection power is not considered a property of the experimental design. In this talk I revisit what statistical power really means, why it belongs to the planning stage rather than the analysis, and why the textbook *t*-test breaks down on data that are skewed, riddled with missing values, and spread across thousands of simultaneous tests, as phosphoproteomics data invariably are. I then introduce two companion R packages from my group. The first, peppwR, replaces closed-form power formulae with simulation that respects real data distributions, structured (MNAR) missingness, and multiple-testing correction. The second, pepdiff, calls differential abundance on the resulting data using the same statistical realities and the same family of tests. Drawing on real DDA (*Arabidopsis*) and PRM (*Magnaporthe*) phosphoproteomics datasets, I show that typical discovery designs power only a small fraction of the peptidome, and that honest power analysis can tell you the sample size you actually need before you spend the money. Although the examples are drawn from proteomics, the statistical thinking and the tools apply to essentially any experiment in the MPMI field and beyond.
